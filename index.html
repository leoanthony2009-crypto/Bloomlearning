AI supports. Humans lead.

AI may help an educator think. It must not become the person who diagnoses a child, decides whether abuse has occurred, makes safeguarding decisions, determines a child's support plan — or replaces the trusted adult.

Who this course is for

Teachers, pastoral leads, heads of year/form, guidance officers, school leaders and any educator with pastoral responsibilities in Trinidad & Tobago and the wider Caribbean.
Opening question
Where could AI genuinely help pastoral staff — and where should it never take over?

Think about one pastoral task that takes time, care or careful language. Would AI help you prepare — or would it risk replacing a human responsibility?

Learning outcomes

LO1Identify at least three appropriate ways generative AI can support pastoral work.
LO2Recognise key risks: inaccurate output, bias, privacy, inappropriate disclosure and over-reliance on AI.
LO3Apply the PAUSE framework before using AI for a pastoral-related activity.
LO4Distinguish AI-assisted preparation from situations requiring human professional judgement, safeguarding or specialist support.
LO5Use POUI to work through a fictional pastoral scenario without entering identifiable or sensitive student information.

What generative AI can do

Generative AI predicts and produces responses based on patterns in information. It is useful for words, ideas and practice — not for truth, diagnosis or safeguarding decisions.

Draft

Questions, check-in scripts, letters

Reframe

Tone, clarity and language

Generate

Fictional scenarios and resources

Reflect

On assumptions and options

AI is a drafting and reflection tool. It is not a professional judgement system.

What AI cannot know

Fluent language is not the same as truth, context or wisdom. However confident the output sounds, the AI does not:

  • know the child, the family, the school or the community;
  • know the current level of risk;
  • verify every fact it states;
  • possess professional safeguarding judgement.

Its output may be wrong, biased, incomplete or overconfident. It does not replace Student Support Services, guidance/counselling, safeguarding leads or specialist support.

Confident-sounding is not the same as correct.

Reflect
Which part of PAUSE do you think educators are most likely to forget when AI feels convenient?

There's no single right answer — but convenience most often erodes P (pasting real details to save time) and U (accepting fluent output uncritically). Notice which one you chose.

Before any prompt: P + A

P

Protect privacy

Never enter into an AI system:

  • student names, or initials that could identify someone
  • student numbers, contact details or images
  • safeguarding records
  • medical or mental-health information
  • trauma histories
  • confidential family or case information
Reminder: removing a name alone may not make a small-community case anonymous. For this course, use fictional or deliberately constructed scenarios only.
A

Ask why AI is needed

Before opening AI, ask: what am I trying to achieve? Good purposes include:

  • preparing questions
  • generating fictional training scenarios
  • improving the tone of a generic communication
  • reflecting on assumptions
  • practising professional judgement
AI should not be used simply because it is available — and never for diagnosis, truth-finding or high-stakes decisions.

After the output: U + S + E

U

Use professional judgement

AI output may be wrong, incomplete, biased, overconfident or unsuitable for your context.

Treat it as a suggestion to evaluate — not a professional decision. Check important information against approved school, Board and Ministry guidance.

S

Safeguard through human systems

For any concern about harm, abuse, neglect, self-harm, serious mental-health risk or violence: follow the school's approved safeguarding and referral process.

Do not ask AI whether a disclosure is true. Do not delay a safeguarding response while consulting AI.

E

Ensure human connection remains central

Pastoral care depends on trust, presence, listening, relationship, context and human responsibility.

AI may support the educator behind the scenes. It must not displace the trusted adult, pastoral lead, Student Support Services, counselling or specialist support.

1

Prepare — not diagnose

AI can suggest non-judgemental questions for a pastoral check-in.

“Generate three gentle, non-judgemental opening questions an educator could use to check in with a student who has recently become disengaged. Do not assume a cause or diagnosis.”

Good output describes observations, invites conversation, avoids labels, preserves choice and keeps the adult responsible.

2

Reflect on assumptions

AI can help surface what is known, assumed and missing — using fictional scenarios only.

“In this fictional scenario, what assumptions might the educator be making? What is actually known, and what should remain uncertain?”
Known facts→Possible explanations→Professional role→Next safe step
3

Improve generic communication

AI can help draft warmer, clearer, partnership-focused wording.

“Draft a warm, concise message inviting a parent or caregiver to discuss concerns about attendance and missed work. Keep it non-judgemental and focused on partnership.”

Before using it: remove identifying details · check accuracy · align with school policy · adapt to the family context. Never paste a real student's details into the prompt.

4

Practise professional judgement

AI can create fictional scenarios in which educators practise deciding whether to:

RESPOND→CONSULT→REFER→SAFEGUARD

The professional decides. AI helps them practise reasoning.
Fictional scenario

A Form 3 student who has usually been engaged has recently:

  • arrived late several times;
  • missed assignments;
  • participated less in lessons;
  • appeared quieter than usual.
A colleague comments:“Something serious must be happening at home.”

Your task: separate facts from assumptions before deciding what to do next. Work with the scenario — never with real student data.

Re-teach: the two non-negotiables

P is Protect privacy. Never enter names, identifying initials, student numbers, contact details, safeguarding records, medical or mental-health information, trauma histories or confidential family information into an AI system. Use fictional scenarios only.

A disclosure goes to the school's approved safeguarding procedure — immediately. Never enter it into AI, never ask AI to assess its seriousness, and never wait for more information before acting.

AI can help

  • prepare check-in questions
  • generate fictional scenarios
  • improve generic communications
  • create check-in resources
  • surface assumptions for reflection

AI should not
